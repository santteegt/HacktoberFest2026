## Definition

For a 1/10 touring car that is loose or sliding when the driver gets on the throttle out of a corner on a low-grip or dusty asphalt track, the ToniSport episodes recommend softer (thinner) rear gear-diff oil, together with enough rear toe-in. This addition resolves a conflict inside the frozen snapshot, where one page warns that thinner oil can go loose on low grip and another lists softer oil as a rear-traction lever.

## Key Points

- **Loose on power on low grip: go softer on the rear diff oil**: when the car is hard to drive out of corners, the driver is always hard on the gas and the car slides or drifts, the "Gear Diff Talk" episode says to move to a softer diff oil, because it lets the driver apply power earlier on the rear wheels.
- **Pair thinner oil with enough rear toe-in**: the "More Overall Traction" episode says to check rear toe-in first; if it is not enough, use about 3.0 to 3.5 degrees of rear toe-in together with a thinner rear diff oil of roughly 3,000 to 4,000 cSt, then confirm the toe-in is sufficient. That combination is described as gaining rear traction.
- **Oil that is too hard costs forward traction on a dusty track**: heavy throttle out of a corner on a dusty or low-grip track with hard diff oil gives little forward traction; a softer diff makes it easier to find the right throttle balance.
- **Thicker oil is not a side-grip fix**: the notebook's own summary (not a direct episode quote) says harder oil sends torque straight across the axle and does not add side bite on loose surfaces, while it gives stronger exit acceleration on high-grip tracks.
- **Lower confidence: very thin oil can make the apex edgy on high grip**: this trade-off comes from the notebook's own summary, not from a quoted episode line, so treat it as a caution rather than a rule.
- **Interpretation (ours, not stated by the episodes)**: the earlier snapshot warning that thinner oil "can go loose on low grip" most likely describes turn-in and entry looseness, while these episodes address on-power looseness on loose surfaces. Both can be true for the same car.
