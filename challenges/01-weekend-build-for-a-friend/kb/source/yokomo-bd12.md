## Definition

The Yokomo BD12 is the 1/10 scale Modified Touring Car chassis the owner races on outdoor asphalt. It is a refinement of Yokomo's prior BD-series geometry rather than a clean-sheet design, and is the chassis every setup and telemetry baseline in this project should ultimately be checked against.

## Key Points

- **Design changes versus the BD11**, per Yokomo's own chassis overview and confirmed/sharpened by the designer's own developer-comments video: the 90T spur gear (down from the BD11's 110T) has its mounting shaft lowered 3.5 mm specifically, dropping the drivetrain's centre of gravity; a split upper deck around the motor-mount area adds rigidity while enabling that lower gear position. The shock case is shortened 0.5 mm with a lowered upper-cap design, making the whole shock assembly ~2 mm shorter overall, with shock towers lowered to match; front shock mounting steps increased from 3 positions (BD11) to 5, allowing shocks to stand more upright, and the rear shock tower mounts to the rear body stay so rear shock angle is adjusted just by swapping spacers. Spring retainers gained built-in ball bearings — new on the BD12 — so the spring can twist freely as the shock compresses (springs naturally twist under compression) without binding, improving tire grip/feel. Ships standard with 4-hole pistons.
- **Factory baseline alignment** (from the designer's own developer-comments video): front camber 1.5° negative, rear camber 2.5° negative, rear toe-in 3.5°, front toe-out 1.0°, ride height 5.0 mm (both asphalt and carpet), and droop/rebound set so that lifting the chassis at 5.0 mm ride height gives 1.5–2.0 mm of upward extension/rebound travel. These numbers differ somewhat from the chassis-agnostic (Xray-derived) baselines in [Touring Car Setup Procedure](touring-car-setup-procedure.md) — the BD12-specific values above should take precedence for the owner's own chassis, with the general baselines as a fallback starting point where Yokomo doesn't specify. This partially resolves the "not yet done" comparison noted below; a full comparison against real BD12 driver setup sheets is still open.
- **Assembly/maintenance notes from the designer**: when eliminating slop in the anti-roll-bar mount set screws, leave slight play rather than adjusting fully tight — over-tightening makes handling overly sensitive to small track changes (consistent with the general anti-roll-bar guidance in [Touring Car Suspension Tuning](touring-car-suspension-tuning.md)). Rear body-mount post spacing is 106 mm (53 mm off centerline); cut the body's molded rear cut line first, then align Yokomo's body-mount reinforcement patches to that line before reaming, to avoid the reamer slipping or tearing the shell.
- **The BD11 predecessor** (Yokomo's prior-generation chassis, covered in depth by a five-part build series but not itself owned by the project) introduced several structural ideas the BD12 either kept or refined: graphite suspension arms pivoting on chassis-mounted pivot balls rather than molded plastic arms and hinge pins (removing unsprung pin slop); a hinged rear top deck with a two-axis floating O-ring/bearing pivot, tunable by O-ring shore hardness or locked with an optional brace; independent left/right front shock towers bolted to each bulkhead for free bulkhead flex; anti-roll bars mounted underslung on the chassis plate beneath the suspension arms (lower CG, faster bar engagement); and a notably soft standard rear spring rate (2.0–2.3 progressive) driven by its lower CG and longer-arm leverage ratio. General BD-series build technique (differential assembly, bulkhead squaring, composite-parts prep) carries forward to the BD12 and is recorded in [Touring Car Drivetrain Tuning](touring-car-drivetrain-tuning.md) and [Touring Car Suspension Tuning](touring-car-suspension-tuning.md).
- **Specification gaps, partly filled from Yokomo's own drawings**: the chassis overview page gives no dimensions, weight, differential type, gear ratio range, kit contents, or exact release date beyond "2023-24 series". The setup sheet's orthographic CAD drawings, measured with a millimetre grid, now give approximate geometry without the manual: a 260–262 mm wheelbase, ~192 mm over the tyres, and the full component layout. Weight, gear ratio range and kit contents still need the manual or the owner's own kit. See [Yokomo BD12 Layout and Hardpoints](sources/yokomo-bd12-layout-from-setup-sheet.md).
- **Component layout (from the setup-sheet top view)**: motor mid-left, can across the car, endbell outboard; spur on the centreline just behind mid-wheelbase, on a layshaft driving two centreline belts; ESC with fan rear-left; receiver and steering servo left; a full-length battery lengthwise on the right, with the chassis plate widened on the right to carry it. Shocks sit on independent left/right carbon towers, the front ones about 14–17 mm ahead of the front axle and the rear ones 14–19 mm behind the rear axle. The front steering arms trail the axle. The lower-arm inner hinges sit about 18–20 mm off centre and low on the plate, and the camber links' inner balls sit on the bulkhead tops about 40 mm up. This left-heavy electronics and right-side battery split is the baseline any [left/right weight balance](touring-car-weight-balance.md) work on this chassis starts from.
- **Shock assembly procedure** (from designer Naoya Kitagawa): assemble the cylinder and fill with oil, attach the resin shock cap to the aluminium upper cap and verify it's firmly seated (called out as an easy step to get wrong), tighten the upper cap, then install the bearing on the shock end — pulling the spring cup down and pressing the shock end in from above if the bearing is difficult to seat, rather than forcing it. The diaphragm inside the shock cylinder must be accounted for during fill. No oil weight, piston hole count, or spring rate figures are given in this procedure — those come from the manual or driver setup sheets.
- **Official resources**: an editable setup-sheet PDF template (saved locally at `assets/BD12_EditableSetupSheet.pdf`) and the official Yokomo manual PDF.
- **The setup-sheet PDF's field structure has been fully reverse-engineered** (see [BD12 Setup Sheet: PDF Field-Mapping Findings, and the OpenGrid Setup-Sheet App](sources/bd12-setup-sheet-app.md)): 108 AcroForm fields with no embedded text layer, no native radio groups, and — the key finding — most printed either/or rows (Cams High/Low, Wheel Hubs 4.0/4.5/5.0mm, and similar) wired with only ONE real checkbox rather than one per visual option. This is now the basis of `apps/setup-sheet`, a chassis-first PWA for filling and reading setup sheets offline; its schema is the authoritative field map going forward.
- **Real driver setup sheets exist for this exact chassis**, split by surface — asphalt entries include Benjamin Brasset (French Nationals, Montpellier, 2024-03) and the chassis's own designer Naoya Kitagawa (Green Park, high traction, 2023-07); several drivers (Kitagawa, Tsubasa Yoshimura, Enrico Jung) appear on both asphalt and carpet sheets, making them a useful control group for separating surface-driven from driver-preference-driven setup deltas.
- **Shares most of its architecture and configuration with the prior BD11** (per petitrc's chassis notes, and consistent with the BD11 predecessor detail above) — the BD12 field map is a reasonable starting point for a BD11 template in `apps/setup-sheet` later, not a one-off.
- **Not yet done**: pulling actual droop/camber/toe/gearing/shock-oil numbers from real BD12 driver sheets (as opposed to the factory baseline above, which is now known) and comparing them against the chassis-agnostic (Xray-derived) baselines in [Touring Car Setup Procedure](touring-car-setup-procedure.md), to see how much generic advice transfers versus how much is chassis-specific — the same baselines currently seed `apps/setup-sheet`'s soft validation advisories, unverified against this chassis specifically. A handful of the sheet's fields (the Battery Position grid, a couple of unlabeled diagram brackets) also remain unnamed with confidence — would need the full manual or a filled reference sheet to resolve.
